1 The product

One shared "brain" (the software) that can live in different bodies (the device you choose). It captures what you hear, remembers it, and answers in plain Uzbek/Russian — across four use cases, built in order of value and feasibility.

Launch

Conversation memory

“What did we agree to ship by Friday?” Records meetings & talks, answers questions, and gives a morning digest.

Add-on

Screen / digital memory

“What was that link I saw yesterday?” Remembers what crossed your phone & PC — local-only, default off.

Phase 2

Find-my-things

“Where did I last see my keys?” Needs a camera — returns the moment & place it last saw the object.

Phase 3

Show-me-how

“How did I do this last time?” The hardest — replays the steps it watched you take before.

Strategy in one line: software-first & audio-first. Prove the memory engine with a cheap audio pendant, win on Uzbek quality + trust, and let the subscription — not the hardware — be the business.

2 The business model

A razor-and-blades / membership model — the same one that built Oura (~$11B) and Whoop (~$10.1B). Cheapest possible way in; durable, recurring subscription revenue.

Hardware at cost

Device sold at manufacturing + expenses — no profit. It exists to start the habit. Lowest barrier in the market; pay with BNPL (Uzum/Click).

Subscription = the business

All margin from a monthly fee, billed via Payme/Click. High margin once transcription is in-country.

First 3 months free

A long runway to build the daily habit. The flip side: free-to-paid conversion is the #1 metric — so we bill annually and auto-convert.

Transparency de-risks us. Oura got a class-action for hiding a subscription. "Device at cost, value is the service" is an honest story that fits a price-sensitive market.
Beat churn like Whoop: bill annually upfront. AI apps keep only ~21% of users at 12 months — collecting a year ahead locks users past the risky early months.

3 Why it's profitable — and defensible

A heavy user produces ~90 hours of speech a month. Transcribing that in the cloud costs more than the subscription. Elsewhere you fix this on the phone — but Uzbek budget phones can't, and Uzbek is a low-resource language. The Uzbek constraints force a better answer.

LTV vs. the churn risk

AI apps keep only ~21% of users at 12 months; ~50% of wearables are abandoned within a year. Because hardware is sold at cost, a converted user repays the 3 free months in ~1–2 paid months — so the levers are conversion + early retention, defended by annual prepay and the daily briefing.

7 Privacy, trust & the law

An always-on recorder is radioactive if done wrong — and in Uzbekistan, doing it right is also legally required and a brand asset. (Research synthesis, not legal advice — use local counsel.)

Voiceprints stay in Uzbekistan — biometric data localization (2026 rule).
Register the database with the State Personalization Center.
Default OFF, capped/tap-to-record, clear recording indicator.
Explicit consent — for the user and a defensible bystander stance.
Encrypt everywhere + one-tap delete.
No emotion/biometric profiling.
"Your voice stays in Uzbekistan, encrypted" — a headline, not fine print.
Derived text/memory may go abroad (post-2026 reform); keep a local fallback.
