1 What Omnisense is

One shared "brain" (the software) that can live in different bodies (the device you choose). It captures what you hear, see, and do, remembers it, and lets you retrieve or be reminded of anything. It does this through four use cases — built in order of value and feasibility.

MVP

Conversation memory

“What did we agree to ship by Friday?” Records meetings & talks, then answers questions and gives a morning digest.

Early add-on

Screen / digital memory

“What was that link I saw yesterday?” Remembers what crossed your phone & PC — local-only, default off.

Phase 2

Find-my-things

“Where did I last see my keys?” Needs a camera — returns the moment & place it last saw the object.

Phase 3

Show-me-how

“How did I do this last time?” The hardest — replays the steps it watched you take before.

What it is NOT: not a phone replacement (that killed Humane & Rabbit), not an emotional "AI friend" (that's a different, backlash-prone product), and not "everything uploaded to our cloud." It augments your phone and keeps your data yours.

2 The strategy, in four decisions

Tailored to a solo builder who wants to learn fast. Each decision is the direct lesson of who lived and who died in this market.

1 · Software-first — don't build hardware yet

Every standalone gadget failed. As a solo builder, custom electronics are the fastest way to burn time and money. Prove the memory + recall engine on hardware you can buy today (your phone). Build a device only if demand is undeniable.

2 · Audio-first — not video

“Remember my conversations” is the proven sticky use case (it made PLAUD #1). It's cheap to run, far less legally fraught than filming bystanders, and the weak spot everyone shares — knowing who said what — is your chance to be better.

3 · Glasses are the north-star device

Camera at eye level = true first-person view; audio in/out built in; socially accepted. But that's a later bet. Note: Meta's glasses are a closed platform — for DIY vision you'd use open hardware (Omi, Brilliant Labs) or the phone.

4 · Win on trust, not scale

Meta bought Limitless and Amazon bought Bee in late 2025. You can't out-scale them — only out-trust them. Local-first, encrypted, "your memories stay yours" is the most credible edge a small independent has.

4 Ten design rules (earned in blood)

Distilled from the wins and failures above. These are guardrails, not suggestions.

1

Augment the phone — never replace it. Lean on its compute, screen, and connectivity.

2

One killer use case, done reliably beats a grand "does everything" vision.

3

Battery & heat are existential (if you ever build hardware). Full day or don't ship.

4

Latency kills voice UX. Aim sub-second; do cheap things on-device.

5

Never overpromise the AI. Demo only what reliably works.

6

Summaries & action items are the feature — not raw transcripts.

7

Default OFF, opt-in, on-device by default. The #1 lesson from Microsoft Recall.

8

Consent is part of the product — recording indicator + bystander consent flow.

9

Production-grade security at launch. No secrets in client code (Rabbit's sin).

10

Plan for offline / degraded modes so the thing still has value if a server dies.

Three tiers: do cheap/private work close to you, heavy work in the cloud

Capture (buy / hack, don't build)

Your phone or AirPods for audio; an open pendant (Omi) if you want hands-free; an open camera device or the phone for vision later. Screen via the open-source screenpipe.

Edge — your phone / laptop

Skip silence (voice-activity detection), buffer locally & encrypted, optional on-device transcription and private-info redaction for sensitive moments.

Cloud — your backend (the part you actually build)

High-accuracy speech-to-text + speaker ID, a memory store (Postgres + vectors), a knowledge graph of people/commitments, and a frontier LLM (e.g., Claude) that answers over your memory.

The 24/7 reality check: always-on capture means cost and storage add up fast. Skipping silence, summarizing, and "rolling up" old data aren't optional — they're what make it affordable. (This is also why continuous video waits until later.)

6 Privacy & legal — the foundation

This is the part that sinks always-on devices when done wrong. Treat it as a first-class feature, not an afterthought. (This is research synthesis, not legal advice — get a lawyer before any public launch.)

The Recall lesson

Microsoft's screen-memory feature stored everything in a plaintext database. Researchers extracted months of activity. Huge backlash → year-long redesign. Local storage ≠ security.

Recording-consent law

~11–12 US states need all-party consent (CA, IL, FL, PA, WA…). Recording non-consenting people there can be a crime. Voiceprints/faces are biometric data (Illinois BIPA = class-action magnet).

EU rules

Recording strangers makes you a data controller under GDPR. The EU AI Act bans emotion recognition & biometric categorization (fines up to €35M / 7%). So: no mood/emotion features.

Build these in from day one

Default OFF — recording is opt-in, never on out of the box.
On-device by default — cloud only with explicit, revocable opt-in.
Encrypt everywhere — at rest & in transit; gate access behind biometrics/PIN.
Clear recording indicator — hard to defeat; no silent-record mode.
Bystander consent — capture only the owner's voice by default; others opt in.
Sensitive-content filtering — exclude passwords, finance, health; private zones.
Short retention + one-tap delete — honor erasure (GDPR/CCPA/BIPA).
Regional feature-gating — limit features in all-party states & the EU.
Turn privacy into the brand. Since Big Tech now owns the big memory wearables, “your memories stay yours — local-first, encrypted, you hold the keys” is the most credible thing a small independent can say.

7 The roadmap (solo, realistic)

Each phase has a gate. Don't advance until it's met. Early phases are about learning, not shipping.

Week 1–2 · Phase 0

The Spike — “Does this even help me?”

Record one real meeting → transcribe + identify speakers → ask questions about it. A few hundred lines of code, no polish.

Gate: You personally find the answers genuinely useful.
Week 3–8 · Phase 1

Personal daily-driver MVP — “I use it every day”

Real capture loop, memory store, a query box, and a morning digest. Privacy basics baked in. Then dogfood it for weeks.

Gate: You'd be annoyed to lose it.
Month 3–5 · Phase 2

Private alpha + first vision

Add screen memory and the first camera use case (find-my-things). Invite 5–20 trusted people (with consent).

Gate: Several other people use it weekly and would miss it.
Month 6–12 · Phase 3

Decide the real product

Sharpen diarization (your edge), attempt “show-me-how,” and decide if there's a business. Only now consider hardware — by forking the open-source Omi design.

Gate: Real demand + a defensible angle (trust, a niche, or diarization quality).
