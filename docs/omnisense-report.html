1 The product

One shared "brain" (the software) that can live in different bodies (the device you choose). It captures what you hear, see, and do, remembers it, and answers in plain language — across four use cases, built in order of value and feasibility.

Launch

Conversation memory

“What did we agree to ship by Friday?” Records meetings & talks, answers questions, and gives a morning digest.

Add-on

Screen / digital memory

“What was that link I saw yesterday?” Remembers what crossed your phone & PC — local-only, default off.

Phase 2

Find-my-things

“Where did I last see my keys?” Needs a camera — returns the moment & place it last saw the object.

Phase 3

Show-me-how

“How did I do this last time?” The hardest — replays the steps it watched you take before.

The strategy in one line: software-first & audio-first. Prove the memory engine with a cheap audio device, win on trust, and let the subscription — not the hardware — be the business. (Glasses are a later premium tier.)

2 The business model

A razor-and-blades / membership model — the same one that built Oura (~$11B) and Whoop (~$10.1B). Give people the cheapest possible way in; earn durable, recurring, high-margin subscription revenue.

Hardware at cost

The device is sold at manufacturing + expenses — no profit. It exists only to get the app onto your body and start the habit. Lowest barrier in the category.

Subscription = the business

All margin comes from a monthly fee (~$12, undercutting the $19 incumbents). On-device processing keeps it ~80% margin.

First 3 months free

A long runway to build the daily habit before charging. The flip side: free-to-paid conversion is the #1 metric to nail.

Transparency de-risks us. When Oura quietly added a subscription and gated features, buyers felt cheated → a 2026 class-action. Our "device at cost, value is the service" framing turns that into an honest, sympathetic story.
Beat churn like Whoop: bill annually upfront and tie the at-cost device to an annual commitment. The category churns hard — collecting a year in advance front-loads cash and locks users past the risky early months.

3 Why it's profitable

The whole model hinges on one engineering choice. A heavy user generates ~90 hours of speech a month. Transcribing that in the cloud would cost more than the subscription. Doing it on the phone collapses the cost — and protects privacy at the same time.

7 Privacy & trust — the foundation

An always-on recorder is radioactive if done wrong (Microsoft Recall's plaintext-database meltdown is the cautionary tale). For us, doing it right is also the brand and the moat. (Research synthesis, not legal advice — counsel before any public launch.)

Default OFF — opt-in, never on out of the box.
On-device by default — cloud only with explicit opt-in.
Encrypt everywhere + biometric/PIN to access.
Clear recording indicator + bystander consent.
One-tap delete & short retention (GDPR/CCPA/BIPA).
No emotion/biometric profiling (banned in the EU).
Regional gating — all-party-consent states & the EU.
"We can't read your memories" — a headline, not fine print.
